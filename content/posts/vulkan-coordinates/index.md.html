# Coordinates and Perspective Projection in Vulkan ## Coordinates I see many posts online discussing whether Vulkan uses right-handed or left-handed coordinates system. But the truth is no where in the Vulkan specification are the words "right hand" or "right-hand" found. Vulkan simply doesn't define a right-handed or left-handed coordinates. Instead, it defines a series of transformations that convert points in clip coordinates to normalized device coordinates (NDC), then to the final framebuffer coordinates which is defined as: "a coordinate system in which adjacent pixels’ coordinates differ by 1 in x and/or y, with (0,0) in the upper left corner and pixel centers at half-integers." ![vulkan framebuffer coordinates](vulkan-coordinates-000-framebuffer-coordinates.png) Normalized device coordinates is defined as: "a coordinate space after perspective division is applied to clip coordinates, and before the viewport transformation converts them to framebuffer coordinates." Below is from Vulkan specification 33.9. Controlling the Viewport on how to convert from NDC to framebuffer coordinates. > The viewport transformation is determined by the selected viewport’s width and height in pixels, > $p_x$ and $p_y$, respectively, and its center ($o_x$, $o_y$) (also in pixels), as well as its > depth range min and max determining a depth range scale value $p_z$ and a depth range bias value > $o_z$ (defined below). The vertex’s framebuffer coordinates ($x_f$, $y_f$) and depth $z_f$ are > given by > > $$x_f = (p_x / 2)x_d + o_x$$ > $$y_f = (p_y / 2)y_d + o_y$$ > $$z_f = p_z \times z_d + o_z$$ Note that no "left handed/right handed" is mentioned. The spec only gives a set of equations. Because $x_f \in [0, p_x]$ and $x_f \in [0, p_x]$, we can conclude $x_d \in [-1, 1]$ and $y_d \in [-1, 1]$. Note, $p_y/2$ is positive, so $+y_d$ goes in the same direction as $+y_f$, downwords. ![normalized device coordinates](vulkan-coordinates-001-ndc-z-question.png) What about z axis? [`VkViewport`](https://docs.vulkan.org/refpages/latest/refpages/source/VkViewport.html) gives more details on that, particularly, on the depth range: $$ o_z = \begin{cases} \mathrm{minDepth},\\[4pt] \dfrac{\mathrm{maxDepth} + \mathrm{minDepth}}{2}, & \text{if } \mathrm{negativeOneToOne} = \mathrm{VK\_TRUE}. \end{cases} $$ $$ p_z = \begin{cases} \mathrm{maxDepth - minDepth},\\[4pt] \dfrac{\mathrm{maxDepth} - \mathrm{minDepth}}{2}, & \text{if } \mathrm{negativeOneToOne} = \mathrm{VK\_TRUE}. \end{cases} $$ where `negativeOneToOne` is defined inside the struct [`VkPipelineViewportDepthClipControlCreateInfoEXT`](https://docs.vulkan.org/refpages/latest/refpages/source/VkPipelineViewportDepthClipControlCreateInfoEXT.html). Also mentioned are the following two notes: > Despite their names, minDepth can be less than, equal to, or greater than maxDepth. > > If the VK_EXT_depth_range_unrestricted extension is not enabled, minDepth must be > between 0.0 and 1.0, inclusive Expand $p_z$ and $o_z$ when `negativeOneToOne!=VK_TRUE`, we get $$z_f = (\text{maxDepth - minDepth}) z_d + \text{minDepth}$$ from which we get $z_d \in [0, 1]$. Similarly, if we expand $p_z$ and $o_z$ when `negativeOneToOne=VK_TRUE`, we get $z_d \in [-1, 1]$. But which direction does +z axis points to, towards the viewer or away from the viewer? To know that we need to decide another setting `depthCompareOp` in [`VkPipelineDepthStencilStateCreateInfo`](https://docs.vulkan.org/spec/latest/chapters/fragops.html#VkPipelineDepthStencilStateCreateInfo). Assume `minDepth=1` and `maxDepth=0`, as a result $z_d \in [0, 1]$ is mapped to $z_f \in [1, 0]$. If `depthCompareOp=VK_COMPARE_OP_LESS`, vertices with smaller $z_f$ value are in front, therefore +z axis should point towards the viewer, if `depthCompareOp=VK_COMPARE_OP_GREATER`, away. ![NDC](vulkan-coordinates-004-ndc-two-z.png width=420) Other combinations of `minDepth`, `maxDepth` and `depthCompareOp` can change +z axis's direction. Let's just use `minDepth=1`, `maxDepth=0` and `depthCompareOp=VK_COMPARE_OP_GREATER` for our example, which gives a _right-handed_ normalized device coordinates. To transform points from clip coordinates ($x_c, y_c, z_c, w_c$) to NDC ($x_d, y_d, z_d$), Vulkan spec 33.7 Coordinate Transformations says: $$ \begin{bmatrix} x_d \\ y_d \\ z_d \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \dfrac{x_c}{w_c} \\[4pt] \dfrac{y_c}{w_c} \\[4pt] \dfrac{z_c}{w_c} \end{bmatrix} $$ Because every element of $x_c$, $y_c$, $z_c$ is divided by the same $w_c$, we cannot change the direction of +z axis without changing the direction of the other two axes, so clip coordinates must have the same "handed-ness" as NDC, "right-handed" in our example mentioned above. ## Perspective Projection Matrix Now the task is to construct a perspective matrix that transforms points in eye/camera coordinates to clip coordinates so that after the perspective division the points in NDC have the ranges: $x \in [-1, 1], y \in [-1, 1], z \in [0, 1]$. Assume we have a left-handed coordinates (different from clip coordinates) for eye/camera space, and the eye space's +z axis goes through the center of the near plane. ![perspective frustum side and top views](vulkan-coordinates-005-pers-frustum-xy.png width=600) $n_x, n_y$ are half-width and half-height of the viewport, and $n, f$ are the z values of the near and far planes. To project ($x_e, y_e, z_e$) onto the near plane, we can use ratio of similar triangles to get $$\frac{y^{\prime}_e}{y_e} = \frac{n}{z_e} \Rightarrow y^{\prime}_e = \frac{n \cdot y_e}{z_e}$$ $$\frac{x^{\prime}_e}{x_e} = \frac{n}{z_e} \Rightarrow x^{\prime}_e = \frac{n \cdot x_e}{z_e}$$ A projected point's x coordinate need to be mapped to [-1, 1] in NDC. Because in our case +y axis points upwrds in eye coordinates (opposite of +y axis in NDC), its y coordinate in eye space need to be mapped to [1, -1] in NDC. ![scaling $y_e$ and $x_e$ to -1,+1](vulkan-coordinates-006-eye-coordinates-xy.png) $$ y_d = \frac{-1 - 1}{n_y - (-n_y)} \cdot y^{\prime}_e = \frac{y^{\prime}_e}{-n_y} $$ $$ x_d = \frac{1 - (-1)}{n_x - (-n_x)} \cdot x^{\prime}_e = \frac{x^{\prime}_e}{n_x} $$ Expand $x^{\prime}_e$ and $y^{\prime}_e$ in terms of $x_e$ and $y_e$, we get: $$ y_d = \frac{n \cdot y_e}{-n_y \cdot z_e} $$ $$ x_d = \frac{n \cdot x_e}{n_x \cdot z_e} $$ To transform from clip coordinates to NDC, both $x_c$ and $y_c$ need to be divided by the same $w_c$. Base on this, we can deduce $w_c = z_e$ and $y_c = \frac{n}{-n_y}\cdot y_e$, $x_c = \frac{n}{n_x}\cdot x_e$. We now can construct the perspective projection matrix partially. $$ \begin{bmatrix} x_c \\ y_c \\ z_c \\ w_c \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \frac{n}{n_x} & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & \frac{n}{-n_y} & 0 & 0 \\ \cdot & \cdot & \cdot & \cdot \\ 0 & 0 & 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x_e \\ y_e \\ z_e \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} $$ To get the third row, we first observe that all points on a plane perpendicular to the near plane should have the same $z_c$, therefore $z_c$ is independent of $x_e$ and $y_e$. Now we have: $$ \begin{bmatrix} x_c \\ y_c \\ z_c \\ w_c \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \frac{n}{n_x} & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & \frac{n}{-n_y} & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & a & b \\ 0 & 0 & 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x_e \\ y_e \\ z_e \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} $$ $$ z_d = \frac{z_c}{w_c} = \frac{az_e + b}{z_e} $$ Because $z_e \in [n, f]$ is mapped to $z_d \in [0, 1]$, we get: $$ \begin{aligned} \frac{a n + b}{n} &= 0 \\[4pt] \frac{a f + b}{f} &= 1 \end{aligned} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \begin{aligned} a &= \frac{f}{f - n} \\[4pt] b &= -\frac{f n}{f - n} \end{aligned} $$ Finally we have our perspective projection matrix: $$ \begin{bmatrix} \frac{n}{n_x} & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & \frac{n}{-n_y} & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & \frac{f}{f - n} & -\frac{f n}{f - n} \\ 0 & 0 & 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix} $$ The point of this exercise is to show you that no matter in which "handed-ness" of coordinate system is your 3D scene, you can construct a perspective matrix that transforms it to the final framebuffer coordinates required by Vulkan (given your choise of `minDepth`, `maxDepth` and `depthCompareOp`). ## Reverse Z and Infinite Far Plane The befinits of using reversed z for depth and infinity for far plane can be found many places online, the following is just for my own memory. Instead of mapping $z_e \in [n, f]$ to $z_d \in [0, 1]$, we map it to $z_d \in [1, 0]$. $$ \begin{aligned} \frac{a n + b}{n} &= 1 \\[4pt] \frac{a f + b}{f} &= 0 \end{aligned} \Rightarrow \begin{aligned} a &= \frac{n}{n - f} \\[4pt] b &= -\frac{f n}{n - f} \end{aligned} $$ $$ \begin{aligned} a = \lim_{f\to\infty}\frac{n}{n - f} &= 0 \\[4pt] b = \lim_{f\to\infty}-\frac{f n}{n - f} &= n \end{aligned} $$ $$ M_{perspective} = \begin{bmatrix} \frac{n}{n_x} & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & \frac{n}{-n_y} & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & n \\ 0 & 0 & 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix} $$ __Note__, since z is reversed in NDC, vertices with greater $z_d$ values should be in front. If we set `minDepth=0` and `maxDepth=1`, $z_d \in [0, 1]$ is mapped to $z_f \in [0, 1]$, we should set `depthCompareOp=VK_COMPARE_OP_GREATER`. ## References: - [List of Vulkan depth related topics](https://docs.vulkan.org/guide/latest/depth.html) - [Visualizing Depth Precision](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/visualizing-depth-precision/) - Nathan Reed - [OpenGL Projection Matrix](https://songho.ca/opengl/gl_projectionmatrix.html) - Song Ho - [The new Vulkan Coordinate System](https://matthewwellings.com/blog/the-new-vulkan-coordinate-system) - Matthew Wellings
